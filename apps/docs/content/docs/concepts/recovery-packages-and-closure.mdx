## What they are

**Recovery** inspects and optionally repairs stale picks, timed-out work, and retryable failures. A **package** is exported JSON containing reusable graph context. **Closure** is the map transition that settles work only after its gates and evidence requirements are satisfied.

## Why they exist

Interrupted sessions are normal. Planr makes recovery previewable instead of guessing from chat history. Packages support local backup and reuse without making a hosted service authoritative. Closure stays evidence-backed rather than becoming a checkbox assertion.

## Who owns them

An operator reviews and applies recovery or imports. Workers provide completion evidence. Reviewers and approvers settle their gates. The map computes whether the full scope is settled.

## How they change

```bash
planr recover sweep --older-than-seconds 900 --json
planr recover sweep --older-than-seconds 900 --apply --json

planr export --include-plans --include-logs --out planr-package.json --json
planr import planr-package.json --preview --json
planr import planr-package.json --confirm --json
```

Preview recovery and imports before applying. Export does not replace the live database; confirmed import restores graph items, links, contexts, logs, plan snapshots, and review artifacts into the current project.

A scope is honestly complete when required items are settled, reviews are complete, approvals are clear, required live verification is logged, and the final summary matches map and evidence. For a stored long-running goal contract:

```bash
planr plan audit <plan-id> --json
```
